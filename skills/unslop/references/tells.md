# Tells

Cut or replace these. Not an exhaustive style guide.

## Content

- Puffery: pivotal, testament, landscape, indelible, deeply rooted.
- Vague crowd: "experts believe", "some critics argue". Name or cut.
- Promotional adjectives: vibrant, stunning, groundbreaking, renowned.
- Formula: "despite challenges, continues to thrive".

## Language

- Stock words: delve, foster, garner, intricate, showcase, underscore,
  tapestry, vibrant, crucial.
- Fancy "is": serves as, stands as, boasts, features.
- "Not just X, but Y." Say the point.
- Forced groups of three. Use the real count.
- Synonym cycling for one thing. Pick one name.
- False ranges: "from X to Y" with no scale.

## Style

- Em dashes. Period or comma.
- Colon as a mid-sentence drumroll.
- Bold on every name. Title Case headings. Decorative emoji.
- Chatbot padding: "happy to help", "great question", "of course".
- Hedged mush: "could potentially possibly".

## Jargon

Abstract nouns that sound technical: substrate, nexus, north star,
flywheel, gold-plating, endgame. Use the concrete word.

## Mannered prose

Figures where a literal phrase exists: aphorisms ("ship it or kill
it"), fragments for effect, code with feelings ("the parser
insists"), figurative verbs ("rides along", "sits on top of"). Say
the literal thing.

## Over-compression

Dropped articles, verbless fragments, arrows, and private
abbreviations make the reader decode. "cfg missing → fallback, warn"
becomes "If the config file is missing, it falls back to defaults and
logs a warning." Terse is fine. Telegraphic is not.

## Plain speech

Say the mechanism or the number, not the feeling. One idea per
sentence. Name the actor. Prefer "use" over "leverage" / "utilize".
